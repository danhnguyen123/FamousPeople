On April 16, 1889, Charles Spencer Chaplin was born in the poorest streets of South London.

His mother, a music hall singer named Hannah Chaplin, lost her voice on stage when Charlie was only five. That night, the boy walked into the spotlight and finished her song.

By the age of nine, he had lived in a London workhouse twice. His mother was committed to an asylum, and his father died of alcoholism in 1901.

In 1913, touring America with the Fred Karno comedy troupe, Chaplin signed his first film contract with Keystone Studios in Los Angeles.

A year later, he stepped in front of the camera in baggy trousers, a tight coat, a bowler hat and a bamboo cane. The Tramp was born.

By 1917, Charlie Chaplin was earning more than a million dollars a year, one of the highest paid people in the world.

In 1919, he founded United Artists with Mary Pickford, Douglas Fairbanks and D. W. Griffith, so that the stars would finally own their films.

The Kid in 1921 and The Gold Rush in 1925 turned him into the most famous face on the planet.
